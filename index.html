For your safety 💛

This app is here to support you gently — it is not a doctor. Because you have PCOS and blood clots, please keep working with a real doctor. Some PCOS treatments and some painkillers aren't safe for people who've had clots, so let your doctor choose what's right for you. See the Move and Ease tabs for warning signs to never ignore.

Eat to feel good

PCOS gets calmer when your blood sugar is steady. The trick is simple and cheap: at every meal, pair protein + vegetables + a little smart carb. Never eat rice or sweets all alone.

The simple rule
  • Add protein (egg, fish, monggo, tokwa) to every meal — it keeps you full and steadies sugar.
  • Fill half your plate with gulay (vegetables). They're cheap and powerful.
  • Keep rice portions smaller, and choose brown/red rice or kamote when you can.
  • Cut sweet drinks first — softdrinks, 3-in-1 coffee, sweet juice. This helps PCOS and weight the most.
  • Drink water through the day. Calamansi in water is a free, lovely swap.

Move, gently

Read first — because of the blood clots

Gentle, regular movement is usually good for circulation. But because you've had blood clots, please ask your doctor before starting any new exercise, and start slow.

Stop and get medical help right away if you notice:

  • Pain, swelling, warmth or redness in one leg (especially the calf)
  • Sudden shortness of breath or chest pain
  • Coughing up blood, or a fast heartbeat with dizziness
The mindset

Forget hard workouts and the gym. You don't need any equipment or money. Consistency beats intensity. A short daily walk does more for PCOS, weight and clots than one tiring session a week.

Start here — walking

Walking keeps your blood moving (good for clots), helps insulin (good for PCOS), and lifts your mood.

  • Begin with 10 minutes a day, around the barangay or in place at home.
  • Build slowly toward 20–30 minutes, most days.
  • If you sit a long time, stand and walk a little every hour — important for clots.

On heavy-cramp days, skip the routine. A slow walk and the gentle stretches in the Ease tab are plenty. Be kind to yourself.

Ready
What helps right now
  • Warmth. A warm compress, water bottle, or a towel soaked in hot water on your lower belly or back.
  • Warm ginger tea. Boil sliced luya in water. Soothing and free.
  • Gentle stretches (below) and slow walking ease the tightness.
  • Hydrate & rest. Drink water, lie on your side with knees up.

About painkillers — please be careful

Because of your blood clots, don't just grab any painkiller. Some pain medicines aren't safe with clots or with blood thinners. Ask your doctor or pharmacist which pain reliever is safe for you. And see a doctor if your cramps are very severe, getting worse, or stopping you from daily life — that's worth checking, especially with PCOS.
